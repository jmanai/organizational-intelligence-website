Definition

Organizational Intelligence

What does that even mean?

An intelligent organization can look at how it works. It can assess itself. It doesn’t blindly follow systems just because “that’s how we do things here”, and then suffocate on its own inefficiency, or find it can’t adapt when things get uncertain.

Notice → Question → Adapt → Evolve

It notices what’s working. It questions what isn’t. And it deliberately changes the way work happens as it evolves, rather than inheriting a system built for a company that no longer exists.

Most organizations never get the chance. The way work happens accumulates: a meeting here, a habit there, a decision that nobody quite made. Nobody chose it, so nobody feels able to change it.

We help organizations choose it.

Point of view

Important ways of working should be chosen or designed deliberately, not inherited.

Not legacy

“We’ve always had this meeting” is not a reason for a meeting to exist.

Not hierarchy

The most senior person in the room is not automatically the right decision-maker for every decision.

Not interpretation

If “good communication” means something different to everyone, it isn’t an agreement yet.

Make the implicit explicit.

Who’s behind it

Yosr Najjar

CEO

Organizational Intelligence comes out of operating, not observing.

Yosr spent years inside a growing organization: building functions, hiring into them, and supporting organizational growth from a small company through 200+ employees and a private acquisition. That included the parts nobody writes case studies about: the meeting that quietly doubled in size, the decision that took three weeks because nobody knew whose it was, the information that only travelled if you were in the right room.

Along the way she designed and led meeting workshops and training, worked with Silicon Valley operating exposure, and built a facilitation practice.

A marketing background turns out to be an unusual advantage in this work. Understanding an audience, communicating clearly, and getting people to actually adopt something are the same problems whether you’re launching a product or changing how a team decides.

To be clear about what this isn’t

This is not HR transformation and it’s not L&D. We’re not here to run a training programme or roll out a framework. We work on how a specific team actually operates, with the people responsible for it.

Approach

Expert and facilitator. Both, on purpose.

There are two familiar failure modes. The consultant who arrives with the answer already written, and the facilitator who has no view at all and just keeps the Post-its moving.

We sit deliberately between them. We bring perspective, structure, tools, patterns we’ve seen before, and challenge. You bring the context, the experience, and the knowledge of your own organization.

Neither of us could produce the answer alone. That’s the point.

We build the answer together.
